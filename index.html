Research

Undergraduate thesis, BRAC University

Bridge

Thesis
Generalized Bridge Pipeline for Multimodal Gene Regulatory Network Discovery
Team
Four authors, including me
Submitted
October 2025
Data
Public HCA, HLCA and DepMap datasets

A pipeline that joins gene expression and CRISPR screen data in one learned space, then uses both to map how genes regulate each other.

Results are as reported in the thesis. These networks have no ground truth, so the checks measure stability and biological plausibility, not accuracy.

  1. The problem
  2. What we built
  3. How we checked it
  4. What it found

The problem

RNA sequencing shows which genes rise and fall together, but not which genes a cell depends on. CRISPR screens show dependency, but not regulation. Network methods built on expression alone miss that functional evidence.

  1. Expression: what moves together
  2. CRISPR: what the cell needs

What we built

Two encoders map each gene's expression profile and CRISPR dependency profile into one shared 128-dimension space. Training lines the two up with an MMD loss, and a diversity term stops everything collapsing to one point. Networks are then built from three signals together: co-expression, GENIE3 regulator importance and CRISPR co-essentiality.

  1. Expression and CRISPR data
  2. Shared 128-d space
  3. Fused gene network

Built with Python, PyTorch, GENIE3

How we checked it

We ran it on blood-forming (hematopoietic) and lung data, and compared each network with an expression-only version: edge overlap (Jaccard), hub ranking (Kendall's tau), how essential the hub genes are in CRISPR screens, and Gene Ontology enrichment.

  1. Two tissues
  2. Stability checks
  3. Biology checks

What it found

The networks kept their structure (Jaccard 0.981 blood, 0.979 lung) and their hub rankings (tau 1.000 and 0.955). Hub genes leaned essential, and enriched for immune and antigen-presentation processes. Blood and lung networks barely overlapped (Jaccard about 0.02), so the wiring stayed tissue-specific. Cross-modal alignment rose from about 0.6 to 0.9 cosine similarity.

Status Reported in the thesis

Convolutional neural networks that classify vegetation in satellite imagery, for environmental monitoring.

  • View the repository
  1. The problem

The problem

Land cover changes faster than anyone can check by hand. Environmental monitoring needs models that can pick out vegetation from satellite images at scale.

What we built

CNN models that classify satellite image patches by land cover, trained on EuroSAT, including a ResNet-50 transfer-learning model. Alongside the classifier, a simple green-pixel rule flags areas where vegetation may have been lost.

  1. Satellite image patches
  2. CNN classifier
  3. Vegetation map

Built with Python, PyTorch, ResNet-50, EuroSAT

The paper

We wrote the work up as "Deep Learning Techniques for Detecting Vegetation with Satellite Imagery". It has been through peer review at Sustainable Futures and is now in revision.

Status In revision

Skin disease detection

Model
EfficientNet-B0, transfer learning, 10 classes
Stack
PyTorch, FastAPI, Streamlit, Gemini Flash
Use
Educational only, not a diagnostic tool

An image classifier for skin conditions with a simple app around it: upload a photo, see the three most likely classes, and get plain-language next steps.

Built for learning and demonstration. It is not a medical device and does not give a diagnosis. A demo video is in the repository.

  • View the repository
  1. What it does
  2. How it works
  3. Handling uncertainty

What it does

Takes a photo of a skin condition and returns the three most likely of ten classes, each with a confidence score, followed by recommendations, next steps and general tips.

How it works

An EfficientNet-B0 model, fine-tuned with transfer learning in PyTorch, serves predictions through a FastAPI backend. A Streamlit front end shows the results, and Gemini Flash writes the guidance around them.

  1. Photo
  2. EfficientNet-B0 via FastAPI
  3. Top 3 classes
  4. Guidance from Gemini

Built with Python, PyTorch, FastAPI, Streamlit, Gemini Flash

Handling uncertainty

It shows three classes instead of one answer, warns when confidence is low, and falls back to built-in guidance when the language model is unavailable. Every result carries an educational-use disclaimer.
